The Highest Form of Knowledge

Social Science 43 · August 1, 2026

“The highest form of knowledge is empathy, for it requires us to suspend our egos and live in another’s world.”
— Bill Bullard

You can know that someone is struggling without understanding how they feel. You can know someone’s beliefs without understanding why they hold them. You can know someone’s story without understanding the person that lived it. Knowledge gives us information about the world, but empathy allows us to see it through the eyes of others. It allows us to truly understand why people feel the way they do, and why things are the way they are. We must look at the roots before we see what is above the surface. It takes the power to forget about ourselves, and live in the world of someone else. This poses a question. Is knowledge really just about knowing things? Or is it about understanding them?

The difference between the two is not always as easy to recognize as it seems. We often believe that knowing all of the facts of a situation allows us to fully understand it. The moment we see someone’s actions, we immediately form an opinion about them, without knowing what led them there. We hear someone’s beliefs and judge them right away, without taking the time to understand the experiences that shaped them. It is much easier to look at a tree and say that it is dying than to figure out why that may be true. Empathy allows us to do something extremely difficult: take a moment before we judge.

Along with this, a common mistake we make is believing that we have to agree with someone in order to understand them. However, that could not be any further from the truth. We all have different experiences, different perspectives, and different walks of life. Someone else’s beliefs can be different from yours, but you can still understand them. They have just experienced life differently from you, and it is still possible to understand that. It is still possible to learn the experiences of others and see why they lead to the way they are today.

In reality, the highest form of knowledge does not include believing things, nor does it include knowing them. It is understanding them, and knowing why they are the way they are. We mustn't just focus on ourselves and what we think, but also that of others. For we are one humanity, and every person has a story we may never fully know. The highest form of knowledge is not simply knowing more about the world, but understanding those who live in it. Thank you for reading Social Science 43, and have a knowledgeable day.
