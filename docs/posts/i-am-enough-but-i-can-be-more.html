I Am Enough, But I Can Be More

Social Science 43 · October 3, 2026

“I am still learning.”
— Michelangelo

Good day to all, and welcome back to Social Science 43. Oftentimes, we lose sight of how far we have come as a result of pondering what we want to be. Looking ahead toward something greater, we blind ourselves from our current position. Not just this, but we forget about what it took to bring us to where we have gotten. Instead of this, we should recognize our accomplishments and strip away the guilt of being proud of them. For some, it may be easy to recognize their accomplishments; for others, it may not be. However, across a vast majority, it is common to overlook the work it took, and focus on where it got us. But why is this?

Perhaps the main reason we overlook our accomplishments is because success has a strange way of becoming ordinary. Something that had seemed difficult at once becomes that which we expect of ourselves rather quickly. A goal that once felt distant becomes our new standard, and we immediately keep looking ahead, rather than looking back at what it took. In this way, our achievements become invisible to us. Not because we cannot see the destination, but because we forget how we got there.

However, it is still correct to look forward and focus on how we can improve. The problem occurs when our wanting for more makes what we already have feel insignificant. We must remember that where we are now is enough, but there is always a reason to want more. There should always be a drive to move forward, even while recalling the past. For what came before us is a guide, not something to be dismissed.

There is a significant difference between believing we are enough and believing we are completely finished. Being enough does not mean that there is nothing left to learn, improve, or accomplish. It simply means that our worth is not defined by reaching a hypothetical version of ourselves. We can appreciate who we are now while still working toward what we aspire to become.

Maybe this is the balance we should strive for. It is important that we be proud enough to recognize how far we have come, yet humble enough to recognize how far we can still go. All of us are just enough right as we are, but that does not mean we cannot become more than this. The past should neither be something we are ashamed of, nor something we are trapped in. It should be a reminder of what we have accomplished, as well as a guide for what comes next. Thank you for reading Social Science 43, and remember that you are enough, but you can be more.
